# Top 10 Beer Halls and Beer Gardens in Munich

Beer is part of daily life in Munich in a way that's hard to match anywhere else. The city's beer halls have been serving its own breweries' beers for centuries, and in summer, locals head to shady beer gardens under chestnut trees, where long wooden tables fill with friends, families and colleagues. It's a tradition that's as much about community as it is about drinking.

This guide covers 10 of the best beer halls and beer gardens in Munich, each with a practical box covering how to get there, how long to spend, the best time to go and one tip. Further down you'll find what to order, how beer gardens work, suggested routes, seasons and festivals, where to stay, getting around and budget guidance.

Opening hours and seasons vary, particularly for beer gardens, which depend on the weather. Check each venue's website before you go.

## 1. Hofbräuhaus

![The vaulted, painted ceiling and long tables of the Hofbräuhaus beer hall](photo:hofbrauhaus-munich)
*Photo: credit on Unsplash*

The Hofbräuhaus is the most famous beer hall in the world. Founded in the 16th century as the royal brewery of the Dukes of Bavaria, it's a huge, noisy hall with vaulted, painted ceilings, long wooden tables and a brass band playing traditional music.

It's undeniably touristy, but it's also an essential Munich experience. Regulars still have their own reserved tables (Stammtisch), some with personal beer mugs locked in a rack by the entrance. The menu is classic Bavarian: roast pork, sausages, pretzels and pork knuckle.

Upstairs is the Festsaal, a grand hall used for events, and there's a courtyard beer garden in summer.

**Practical box**
- **How to get there:** On Platzl in the Altstadt, a short walk from Marienplatz.
- **How long to spend:** One to two hours.
- **Best time to go:** Weekday afternoons are calmer than evenings.
- **Tip:** Seats are shared, so ask "Ist hier noch frei?" (is this free?) before sitting at a table, and avoid reserved Stammtisch tables.

## 2. Augustiner-Keller

![A large beer garden with chestnut trees and long tables at Augustiner-Keller](photo:augustiner-keller-beer-garden)
*Photo: credit on Unsplash*

Many locals consider Augustiner the best of Munich's breweries, and the Augustiner-Keller is one of the city's most loved beer gardens. Its large, leafy garden under chestnut trees seats thousands of people, and on a summer evening it's full of locals relaxing after work.

There's a self-service area where you can bring your own food, as is traditional, and a served area with full table service. Indoors, there's a beer hall and historic cellars for cooler weather.

Augustiner is known for serving some of its beer from wooden barrels (Holzfass), which many drinkers believe gives a smoother taste.

**Practical box**
- **How to get there:** On Arnulfstraße, a short walk from Munich Hauptbahnhof.
- **How long to spend:** Two hours or more on a summer evening.
- **Best time to go:** A warm early evening.
- **Tip:** Look for beer served from the wooden barrel and try it alongside a regular pour.

## 3. Augustiner Bräustuben

![A cosy wood-panelled Bavarian beer hall with traditional food](photo:augustiner-braustuben)
*Photo: credit on Unsplash*

The Augustiner Bräustuben is a traditional beer hall next to the Augustiner brewery, and it's one of the most authentic beer halls in Munich. It's popular with locals and has a cosy, wood-panelled interior, and you may be able to see the brewery's horses in their stables, which once pulled the beer wagons.

The food is classic, hearty and good value, with roast pork, pork knuckle and sausages. Prices tend to be lower than at the more famous beer halls in the centre.

**Practical box**
- **How to get there:** On Landsberger Straße, near the Schwanthalerhöhe U-Bahn station or Hackerbrücke S-Bahn.
- **How long to spend:** One to two hours.
- **Best time to go:** Dinner; it gets very busy in the evening.
- **Tip:** Book a table for groups, especially during Oktoberfest season, when the Theresienwiese is close by.

## 4. Chinesischer Turm

![The wooden Chinese Tower pagoda in a beer garden in Munich's English Garden](photo:chinesischer-turm)
*Photo: credit on Unsplash*

The Chinesischer Turm (Chinese Tower) beer garden is set around a wooden pagoda in the middle of the English Garden, one of the largest city parks in the world. It's one of Munich's biggest and most famous beer gardens, with thousands of seats.

On summer weekends, a brass band often plays from the tower, and the atmosphere is lively and family-friendly. Food stalls sell roast chicken, pretzels, spare ribs and Obatzda, a spiced cheese spread.

Combine your visit with a walk through the park, including a stop at the Eisbach, where surfers ride a standing wave.

**Practical box**
- **How to get there:** In the English Garden, a walk from the Universität or Giselastraße U-Bahn stations, or by bus.
- **How long to spend:** Two hours, plus time in the park.
- **Best time to go:** A sunny weekend afternoon for the full atmosphere.
- **Tip:** Walk to the Eisbach surfers near the southern end of the park before or after your visit.

## 5. Hirschgarten

![Long rows of tables in the large Hirschgarten beer garden in Munich](photo:hirschgarten-munich)
*Photo: credit on Unsplash*

The Hirschgarten is often described as the largest beer garden in the world, with room for thousands of guests. It's set in a former royal hunting ground near Nymphenburg Palace, and a deer enclosure (Hirsch means deer) is still part of the grounds.

It's very popular with local families and feels less touristy than the beer gardens in the centre. There's a large self-service area, a playground and plenty of space to spread out.

Visit Nymphenburg Palace, the summer residence of Bavaria's rulers, and its gardens before heading to the Hirschgarten.

**Practical box**
- **How to get there:** S-Bahn to Laim or Hirschgarten station, or tram to the Romanplatz area.
- **How long to spend:** Two hours or more.
- **Best time to go:** A summer afternoon, ideally after visiting Nymphenburg Palace.
- **Tip:** Bring your own food for the self-service area, as locals do, and buy your drinks there.

## 6. Löwenbräukeller

![The castle-like exterior of the Löwenbräukeller beer hall in Munich](photo:lowenbraukeller)
*Photo: credit on Unsplash*

The Löwenbräukeller is a grand beer hall and beer garden with a castle-like exterior, home to the Löwenbräu brewery, whose lion symbol is one of the best known in Munich. Inside, a huge festival hall hosts events, and the traditional restaurant serves Bavarian food in wood-panelled rooms.

In summer, its beer garden is a lively place to sit outside. It's less crowded with tourists than the Hofbräuhaus, and it's a good option if you're exploring the museums in the Maxvorstadt area nearby.

**Practical box**
- **How to get there:** At Stiglmaierplatz, which has its own U-Bahn station.
- **How long to spend:** One to two hours.
- **Best time to go:** Dinner or a summer evening in the garden.
- **Tip:** Visit the museums of the Kunstareal, such as the Alte Pinakothek, beforehand.

## 7. Paulaner am Nockherberg

![A beer hall with copper brewing kettles and long tables](photo:paulaner-nockherberg)
*Photo: credit on Unsplash*

Paulaner am Nockherberg is the home of the Paulaner brewery, which was founded by monks. It has a beer hall with a small in-house brewery and a large beer garden on a hill above the Isar River.

It's especially famous for the Starkbierfest (strong beer festival) held during Lent, when Paulaner serves its strong Salvator beer, originally brewed by monks to sustain them through fasting. The festival opens with a televised event in which politicians are teased by comedians.

The rest of the year, it's a relaxed spot for beer and Bavarian food, with views over the city from the garden.

**Practical box**
- **How to get there:** In the Au district, reachable by tram or a walk across the Isar from the city centre.
- **How long to spend:** One to two hours.
- **Best time to go:** During Starkbierzeit in spring, or a summer evening in the garden.
- **Tip:** Try the in-house unfiltered beer if it's available, as it's only served here.

## 8. Viktualienmarkt beer garden

![Maypole and market stalls at the Viktualienmarkt in Munich](photo:viktualienmarkt-munich)
*Photo: credit on Unsplash*

The Viktualienmarkt is Munich's famous open-air food market, and in its centre, under the trees beside the maypole, is a beer garden. It's unusual because it serves beers from Munich's six major breweries in rotation, so you can try a different one each time you visit.

It's a perfect stop while exploring the old town. Buy snacks, cheese, sausages or pretzels from the market stalls, then sit down with a beer in the self-service garden.

**Practical box**
- **How to get there:** Next to Marienplatz in the Altstadt.
- **How long to spend:** An hour.
- **Best time to go:** Lunchtime or early afternoon.
- **Tip:** Check the sign to see which brewery's beer is currently being served.

## 9. Seehaus

![The lakeside Seehaus beer garden with boats on the Kleinhesseloher See](photo:seehaus-munich)
*Photo: credit on Unsplash*

The Seehaus beer garden sits on the shores of the Kleinhesseloher See, a lake in the northern part of the English Garden. It's quieter and more relaxed than the Chinesischer Turm, and the lakeside setting is one of the most beautiful in Munich.

You can rent a rowing boat on the lake in summer, and the beer garden has both self-service and restaurant areas. It's a lovely place to end a long walk through the park.

**Practical box**
- **How to get there:** In the northern English Garden, near Münchner Freiheit U-Bahn station.
- **How long to spend:** One to two hours.
- **Best time to go:** Late afternoon or sunset on a summer day.
- **Tip:** Walk from the Chinesischer Turm to the Seehaus through the park to see both.

## 10. Weisses Bräuhaus (Schneider Bräuhaus)

![A tall glass of cloudy Bavarian wheat beer on a wooden table](photo:weissbier-munich)
*Photo: credit on Unsplash*

The Weisses Bräuhaus is the place to try Bavarian wheat beer (Weissbier). Run by the Schneider brewery, which has specialised in wheat beer since the 19th century, it's a traditional beer hall in the old town with a loyal local following.

Its signature beer is Schneider Weisse Tap 7, a classic dark wheat beer, and its Aventinus is a strong wheat beer known as a Weizenbock. The kitchen is known for traditional Bavarian food, including Weisswurst, the white sausage traditionally eaten before noon with sweet mustard and a pretzel.

**Practical box**
- **How to get there:** On Tal, a short walk from Marienplatz.
- **How long to spend:** One to two hours.
- **Best time to go:** Late morning, for a traditional Weisswurst breakfast.
- **Tip:** Peel your Weisswurst rather than eating the skin, as is the Bavarian custom.

## Beer basics: what to order in Munich

- **Helles:** a pale, light lager and Munich's everyday beer.
- **Weissbier (Weizen):** cloudy wheat beer with banana and clove notes.
- **Dunkel:** a dark lager with malty, bread-like flavours.
- **Radler:** beer mixed with lemonade, popular on hot days.
- **Russ:** wheat beer mixed with lemonade.
- **Starkbier:** strong beer brewed for Lent.
- **Mass:** a one-litre mug, the standard measure in beer gardens and at Oktoberfest. Smaller half-litre glasses are also common.

## How Munich beer gardens work

Traditional Bavarian beer gardens are a unique part of Munich life, with their own customs:

- **Bring your own food:** In the self-service area of a traditional beer garden, you're allowed to bring your own food, a custom protected by Bavarian law. You buy your drinks from the beer garden. Tables with tablecloths are usually for table service only.
- **Self-service:** Pick up a tray, choose your food at the counters, and collect your beer from the bar.
- **Mug deposit:** Some beer gardens charge a deposit (Pfand) on mugs, which you get back when you return them.
- **Shared tables:** Tables are shared, so join others if there's space.
- **Weather:** Beer gardens open when it's warm and dry enough, mostly from spring to early autumn.

## Seasons and festivals

- **Starkbierzeit (spring):** The strong beer season during Lent, especially at Paulaner am Nockherberg.
- **Beer garden season (spring to early autumn):** Most beer gardens open fully when the weather is warm.
- **Oktoberfest (late September to early October):** The world's largest beer festival, held on the Theresienwiese. Beer halls and hotels get very busy.
- **Winter:** Beer halls are the place to be, with hearty food and warm interiors.

Check official sources for exact dates each year.

## Suggested routes for 1, 2 or 3 days

**One day:** Start with a Weisswurst breakfast at the Weisses Bräuhaus, stop at the Viktualienmarkt beer garden at lunchtime, and visit the Hofbräuhaus in the afternoon. Finish with dinner at the Augustiner-Keller.

**Two days:** Spend day two in the English Garden, walking from the Eisbach surfers to the Chinesischer Turm and on to the Seehaus.

**Three days:** Visit Nymphenburg Palace and the Hirschgarten, then have dinner at the Augustiner Bräustuben or the Löwenbräukeller.

## Where to stay

- **Altstadt (old town):** close to the Hofbräuhaus, Viktualienmarkt and Weisses Bräuhaus.
- **Around Hauptbahnhof:** convenient for transport and the Augustiner-Keller.
- **Maxvorstadt and Schwabing:** close to the English Garden, museums and the Löwenbräukeller.
- **Ludwigsvorstadt:** near the Theresienwiese, useful during Oktoberfest.

## Getting around

Munich has an efficient public transport system of U-Bahn, S-Bahn, trams and buses. Most of the city centre is walkable, and cycling is a great way to get around, especially to the beer gardens in the English Garden and around Nymphenburg.

## Budget guidance

- **Budget:** Choose beer gardens' self-service areas and bring your own food, as the locals do.
- **Mid-range:** Eat traditional Bavarian food in beer halls and enjoy a few beers.
- **Comfortable:** Book a table in the served areas or a beer hall restaurant, and visit during festivals.

Carry some cash, as some beer gardens and stalls still prefer it.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the most famous beer hall in Munich?

The Hofbräuhaus is the most famous beer hall in Munich and probably the world. It dates back to the 16th century and has a lively atmosphere with live brass band music.

### Can you bring your own food to Munich beer gardens?

Yes, in the self-service areas of traditional beer gardens. It's a long-standing Bavarian custom. Drinks must be bought from the beer garden, and served areas with tablecloths are for table service.

### When are Munich beer gardens open?

Most beer gardens open from spring to early autumn, when the weather is warm and dry. Opening times depend on the weather, so check before you go.

### What is the difference between a beer hall and a beer garden?

A beer hall is an indoor venue serving beer and food, open all year. A beer garden is outdoors, usually under chestnut trees, and is mainly open in the warmer months.

### Is Hofbräuhaus worth visiting?

Yes. While it's touristy, it's an iconic Munich experience with historic architecture, live music and a lively atmosphere. Visit in the afternoon for a calmer experience.

### What beer should you order in Munich?

Start with a Helles, Munich's classic pale lager. Try a Weissbier (wheat beer) and a Dunkel (dark lager) too. If it's hot, order a Radler, which is beer mixed with lemonade.
